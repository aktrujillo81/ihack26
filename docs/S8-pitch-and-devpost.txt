WELL WATCH — PITCH OUTLINE AND DEVPOST DRAFT

PITCH OUTLINE

1. The problem
Rural water points such as wells, hand pumps, taps, and boreholes can fail
without the people able to coordinate a repair finding out quickly. A report
that is not seen or followed up is not useful.

2. The idea
Well Watch lets a person report a water-point problem with a basic phone. An
AI assistant asks a few short questions, then the report becomes a prioritized
complaint that NGO coordinators can review, track, pass on, and mark fixed.

3. Who it serves
- Callers: people using a basic phone, possibly with weak signal and limited
  time. They do not need an account.
- Responders: NGO coordinators who need enough location and urgency context to
  decide what happens next.
- Builders: the team maintaining the system, who need clear contracts,
  privacy safeguards, and simple operations.

4. How it works
- A caller reports the village, a nearby landmark, water-point type, problem,
  people affected, school or clinic impact, and duration.
- The assistant confirms the important details and records unknown answers as
  unknown instead of guessing.
- The system matches the report to a known water point when confident, or
  creates an unverified point and asks for human review when it is not.
- The NGO dashboard shows urgency, reason for priority, location, report
  count, status, and history.
- A responder can record that a complaint was passed on and later mark it
  fixed or reopen it.

5. What makes it responsible
- No caller names or phone numbers are stored or shown.
- The product gives no water-safety advice and makes no repair promises.
- Ambiguous locations are marked for review rather than guessed.
- A human confirms every action; nothing is dispatched automatically.
- Color is never the only signal for status or urgency.

6. The ask / vision
Well Watch is a one-district pilot designed to make spoken reports visible,
actionable, and traceable. The data model supports future organizations,
regions, languages, and intake channels without changing the core workflow.


DEVPOST DRAFT

Project name: Well Watch

Tagline:
Turn a phone call about a broken water point into a visible, prioritized,
trackable report for NGO coordinators.

Inspiration:
Rural water infrastructure can fail long before the people who can coordinate
a response hear about it. We wanted to make reporting possible for someone
with a basic phone, while giving responders enough context to decide what to
do next.

What it does:
Well Watch accepts a phone report about a well, hand pump, tap, or borehole.
The voice assistant collects a location description, the problem, impact, and
duration. The report is turned into a complaint that NGO staff can view in a
web dashboard. The dashboard shows priority, why that priority was assigned,
status, location, report count, and history. Staff can record a hand-off to a
partner and mark the complaint fixed.

How we built it:
The web application is built with Next.js and a self-hosted PostgreSQL
database. It is exposed through a Cloudflare Tunnel. Vapi provides the
phone-based voice assistant. The project uses a documented data contract,
server-side access checks, database migrations, and a structured task plan.

Challenges we ran into:
- Designing for callers who may have weak signal, limited time, or only a
  basic phone.
- Capturing useful location information without pretending that an uncertain
  match is correct.
- Protecting caller privacy while keeping reports useful for responders.
- Keeping the phone workflow short while still gathering enough information to
  prioritize a complaint.
- Building a workflow that records a hand-off without promising that a repair
  will happen.

Accomplishments that we are proud of:
- A phone-first reporting path that does not require an app or account.
- Honest unknown and needs-review states instead of silent failures.
- Clear, human-controlled complaint tracking from report through fixed status.
- Privacy and accessibility safeguards built into the design from the start.

What we learned:
We learned that reliable reporting is as much about honest uncertainty and
clear follow-through as it is about collecting data. A system can be useful
without automatically deciding or promising what happens next.

What's next for Well Watch:
- Validate the flow with partner organizations and responders.
- Add more languages after testing the English pilot.
- Add supported intake channels such as a public web form.
- Improve water-point verification and map placement tools.
- Add organization onboarding, filters, analytics, and carefully governed
  exports.

DEMO ACCURACY NOTE

Real in the demo: the phone conversation, structured extraction, the report
pipeline, complaint creation, scoring, accounts and roles, dashboard, map, and
history.

Recorded but not a real external message: passing a complaint to a partner.
It records who and when; it does not contact that partner.

Not built: notifying the caller, automatic dispatch, real repair-provider
integrations, water-safety advice, and a public map.
